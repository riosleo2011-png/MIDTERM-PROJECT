Harlem Plaza Redesign

Reimagining Montefiore Square as a safer, greener, and more welcoming public space for residents, visitors, and neighborhood activity.

Explore the Project

About the Project

This project reimagines Montefiore Square as a safer, greener, and more welcoming public space for everyday community life. Located in Harlem at the intersection of Broadway, West 138th Street, and Hamilton Place, the plaza is highly visible and heavily used — yet most people move through it rather than stay in it.

The redesign explores how the plaza could better support comfort, social activity, and neighborhood identity through improved shade,flexible seating, planting, lighting, and public gathering space. Using site analysis, visual research, and AI-assisted concept development, the project compares multiple design directions before combining them into a final hybrid proposal.

The goal is to transform Montefiore Square from a pass-through space into a flexible public destination that supports students, residents, commuters, and community activity throughout the day.

Site / Context

Montefiore Square is a triangular public plaza located at the intersection of Broadway, West 138th Street, and Hamilton Place in Harlem. The site is surrounded by residential buildings, local businesses, and nearby transit, making it a highly visible and accessible public space within the neighborhood.

The physical layout of the plaza is defined by a combination of open paved areas and raised planting zones. A central circular green space with tiered stone seating creates a focal point, while surrounding pathways guide pedestrian movement through the site. The overall layout is open and easy to navigate, but it also leaves large areas exposed and undefined.

The plaza is used by a mix of pedestrians, including commuters passing through, local residents, and nearby students. Movement through the space is generally continuous, with people walking along the paved paths rather than stopping for extended periods. While the site supports circulation effectively, it provides fewer opportunities for people to comfortably stay and use the space.

Although the plaza benefits from its central location and open design, it lacks stronger spatial definition and areas that encourage gathering. The combination of open pavement, limited shade, and fixed seating results in a space that functions more as a passageway than a destination, creating an opportunity for redesign to improve comfort, activity, and overall usability.

The Problem

The existing plaza lacks shade, seating variety, visual identity, and a clear sense of comfort for everyday use. Large areas of open pavement are exposed to direct sunlight, making the space feel harsh and uninviting, especially during warmer parts of the day. This limits how long people are willing to stay and reduces the overall usability of the plaza.

The space is dominated by wide, empty paved areas with very few places designed for people to gather or spend time. As shown in the image, most users pass through the plaza rather than stopping, which suggests that the current layout does not encourage activity or social interaction. The lack of defined zones for seating, gathering, or relaxation makes the plaza feel underused despite its central location.

Seating options are limited and not flexible. There are few comfortable places to sit, and the existing seating does not allow users to choose where to position themselves, such as in shade or near others. This reduces the plaza’s ability to support a variety of users, including students, families, and older residents who may need more comfortable and adaptable seating.

The plaza also lacks a strong visual identity. The materials and layout feel uniform and generic, without distinctive features that reflect the surrounding Harlem community. Without elements such as public art, varied materials, or culturally meaningful design, the space does not stand out as a memorable or engaging public place.

Together, these issues contribute to a space that feels more like a walkway than a destination. Although the plaza is located in a busy and accessible area, it does not currently provide the comfort, flexibility, or activity needed to encourage people to stay, interact, and fully use the space.

Design Options

Option 1: Green Wellness Plaza

This concept introduces more planting, shade trees, flexible seating, and a calmer atmosphere focused on rest, wellness, and everyday use.

This option would serve students, residents, seniors, and families but offers less event space.

Option 2: Community Activity Plaza

This concept emphasizes gathering, identity, and flexible space for events and social interaction.

It creates energy and activity but offers less everyday calm and shade.

Final Proposal

The final proposal combines both calm and active design strategies into one flexible plaza.

It introduces greenery, seating variety, and a central flexible event space.

The design improves comfort, identity, and usability throughout the day.

Budget & Timeline

This project is designed in phases: quick seating/shade upgrades first, then planting and lighting, then long-term programming.

Success Metrics

Success is measured by increased dwell time, more seating use, and higher community activity.

Reflection / Conclusion

AI helped generate ideas and visual concepts, but final decisions required human judgment.

The final design balances comfort, activity, and identity for the community.
