Harlem Plaza Redesign

Reimagining Montefiore Square as a safer, greener, and more welcoming public space for residents, visitors, and neighborhood activity.

Explore the Project

About the Project

This website presents an urban plaza redesign proposal for Montefiore Square in Harlem. The goal is to improve the site’s comfort, accessibility, safety, and overall identity while creating a stronger space for community use.

The redesign uses site analysis, visual research, and AI-assisted concept development to generate and compare different design directions before selecting a final proposal.

The Problem

The existing plaza lacks shade, seating variety, visual identity, and a clear sense of pedestrian comfort. It feels underused and does not fully support the surrounding neighborhood’s social and public life.

This redesign responds to those issues by focusing on public comfort, circulation, greenery, and a stronger sense of place.

Site / Context

Montefiore Square sits in a busy Harlem context shaped by pedestrian movement, nearby businesses, transit activity, and neighborhood gathering patterns. Site analysis focused on access, circulation, comfort, and opportunities for social use.

Final Proposal

The final proposal combines the strongest qualities of both design directions by creating a plaza that supports both everyday comfort and active community use. Instead of choosing between a calm green space and a social gathering space, the design organizes the plaza so that different areas serve different functions at the same time.

As visitors enter the plaza from the subway side, the space immediately feels more open and inviting, with clearer pathways guiding movement while still encouraging people to stay. The edges of the plaza are redesigned with expanded planting beds, including trees, shrubs, and flowering plants that create a greener and more comfortable environment. These planted areas help reduce noise from the surrounding streets and provide shade, making the space more usable during the day.

Seating is arranged throughout the plaza using moveable café chairs and tables, allowing people to choose where and how they sit. Some seating is placed near planting areas for a quieter experience, while other areas are more open and social. This flexibility allows students, families, and residents to use the space in different ways, whether they are studying, relaxing, or meeting with others.

At the center of the plaza, a flexible open space is maintained to support community activity. This area can be used for everyday gathering, but it also allows for events such as markets, performances, or local programming. By keeping this space open, the plaza can adapt to different needs throughout the day and week without feeling overcrowded or empty.

The design also strengthens the identity of the plaza through visual elements such as warmer paving materials, organized seating layouts, and the potential for public art. These features help the space feel more connected to the surrounding community rather than like a generic urban plaza.

In the evening, improved lighting helps extend the usability of the space and makes it feel safer and more welcoming. Overall, the final proposal creates a balanced environment that supports both quiet everyday use and active social life, making Montefiore Square a more functional and meaningful public space.

Reflection / Conclusion

AI helped me quickly explore different visual directions and test multiple design ideas for the plaza. It was especially useful for comparing how planting, seating, and activity could change the atmosphere of the space. At the same time, the final design decisions still depended on my own judgment about what best fit the site and community.
