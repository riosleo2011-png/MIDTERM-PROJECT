Community Context

Montefiore Square serves a diverse group of users including local residents, City College students, commuters, and families. The nearby subway station and surrounding buildings create a steady flow of people throughout the day.

Students use the space between classes, commuters pass through during peak hours, and residents use the plaza for everyday activities. A successful redesign must respond to all of these users by providing both comfortable seating and opportunities for social interaction.

Budget & Timeline

This proposal can be implemented in phases. Initial improvements such as moveable seating, shade structures, and signage can be completed quickly, while longer-term elements like planting, lighting, and public art can be added over time.

  • Moveable seating: $18,000–$28,000
  • Shade structures: $30,000–$55,000
  • Public art / mural: $40,000–$75,000
  • Lighting: $10,000–$20,000
  • Planting improvements: $35,000–$60,000

Success Metrics

The success of the redesign would be measured by increased activity and longer use of the space. This includes more people staying in the plaza, increased seating use, and more frequent community events.

Additional indicators include improved shade coverage, greater evening use, and stronger community engagement through programming and public art.

Reflection / Conclusion

AI played an important role in the development of this project by helping me quickly explore and visualize different design possibilities for Montefiore Square. I used AI image generation tools to create before-and-after renderings of the plaza, as well as ChatGPT to help organize ideas and improve written sections. Through multiple iterations, I tested different prompts to compare how changes in greenery, seating, and activity could transform the space.

One of the main strengths of AI in this project was speed and visualization. It allowed me to generate several design options and compare them side by side without needing to fully draw or model each version manually. This made it easier to experiment with different levels of planting, shade, and seating arrangements, and to better understand how those changes would affect the overall atmosphere of the plaza.

However, AI also had limitations. Many of the generated images looked visually appealing but felt generic and not specific to Harlem or Montefiore Square. Some designs were unrealistic in terms of how people would actually use the space, or did not fully consider movement, accessibility, or real-world conditions. Because of this, I had to carefully evaluate and reject some of the AI-generated ideas rather than using them directly.

The most important decisions in this project required my own judgment. I had to decide how to balance quiet, everyday use with more active community gathering, and how the design should respond to the needs of students, residents, and visitors. The decision to combine both design options into a hybrid proposal was based on understanding that the plaza needs to support multiple uses at different times of day.

Overall, AI was a useful tool for generating ideas and speeding up the design process, but it was not a replacement for critical thinking. The final proposal reflects a combination of AI-assisted exploration and my own decisions about what would work best for the site and the community.
